# 🧠 COLOR PSYCHOLOGY — HUMAN EMOTION MAPPING
**Every color decision must be justified by human psychology, not aesthetics alone.**

---

## CORE PRINCIPLE

> "Color is the first language the brain speaks. Before reading a single word, a human has already felt an emotion from your palette. This emotion either opens or closes the door to conversion."

---

## EMOTION → COLOR MAPPING

### TRUST & CREDIBILITY
```
Primary: Deep blue #1B4F8A or Navy #0D2B45
Secondary: Clean white / Light gray
Accent: Subtle gold #B8960A for premium signals
Feel: "This company is reliable. I am safe here."
Used by: Financial institutions, healthcare, legal, SaaS B2B
Psychology: Blue activates the prefrontal cortex — rational decision-making
Avoid: Bright saturated colors, busy patterns, warm reds nearby
```

### ENERGY & EXCITEMENT
```
Primary: Electric orange #FF5500 or Vibrant red #E8210A
Secondary: Deep dark background #0A0A0A
Accent: Bright yellow #FFD100 or white
Feel: "This is exciting. I need to act NOW."
Used by: Sports, entertainment, food delivery, gaming
Psychology: Red increases heart rate, stimulates appetite/action impulse
Avoid: Cold blues near primary, muted/desaturated palette
```

### LUXURY & EXCLUSIVITY
```
Primary: Near-black #0A0A0A or Deep charcoal #1A1A1A
Secondary: Warm cream #F5F0E8 or champagne #F4E9D2  
Accent: True gold #B8960A or platinum #D4D4D4
Feel: "This is rare. Not everyone can have this."
Used by: Fashion, jewelry, premium hotels, wine/spirits, private banking
Psychology: Black = power + sophistication; Gold = wealth signal
Avoid: Bright primaries, rounded corners, playful typefaces
```

### INNOVATION & INTELLIGENCE
```
Primary: Electric purple #7C3AED or deep violet #5B21B6
Secondary: Near-black #050508
Accent: Cyan/teal #00D9B8 or electric blue #3B82F6
Feel: "These people are building the future. I want in."
Used by: AI/tech companies, startups, developer tools, creative software
Psychology: Purple = creativity + mystery; paired with dark = premium tech
Avoid: Warm beiges, organic textures, handwritten fonts
```

### HEALTH & NATURE
```
Primary: Forest green #1A4D2E or sage #7D9B76
Secondary: Warm cream #FAF7F2 or natural white
Accent: Terracotta #C17A4A or warm gold
Feel: "This is natural, clean, and good for me."
Used by: Health, wellness, organic food, sustainable brands, yoga
Psychology: Green = growth, safety, natural; signals non-toxic, trustworthy
Avoid: Neon accents, dark heavy backgrounds, aggressive reds
```

### CALM & MINDFULNESS
```
Primary: Muted lavender #9B8EA8 or dusty blue #7B9EB8
Secondary: Off-white #FDFBF7 or warm pearl
Accent: Barely-there sage or pale rose
Feel: "I can breathe here. This is peaceful."
Used by: Meditation apps, mental health, slow fashion, spa
Psychology: Low-saturation palettes reduce cortisol; breathing slows
Avoid: Any saturated color, red/orange anywhere, bold black outlines
```

### PLAYFULNESS & JOY  
```
Primary: Bright coral #FF6B6B or sunny yellow #FFD93D
Secondary: Sky blue #6BCDF7 or mint #4ECDC4
Accent: Purple #A855F7 or lime #84CC16
Feel: "This is FUN. I smile being here."
Used by: Children's products, food/beverage, consumer apps, social platforms
Psychology: Rainbow saturation triggers dopamine; rounded shapes signal safety
Avoid: Dark backgrounds for primary palette, sharp angles, heavy typography
```

### URGENCY & SCARCITY
```
Primary: Deep red #CC0000 or warning orange #FF4500
Secondary: Black or very dark background
Accent: White for maximum contrast on CTA
Feel: "I might miss out. I need to decide NOW."
Used by: Flash sales, limited offers, alerts, deadlines
Psychology: Red + time pressure = fight-or-flight → faster decisions
Avoid: This palette for entire sites — only for specific conversion moments
```

### SOPHISTICATION & EDITORIAL
```
Primary: Pure black #000000
Secondary: Pure white #FFFFFF or warm white #FAFAF8
Accent: Single bold color (red, gold, or electric blue)
Feel: "This is serious. Authoritative. Worth my attention."
Used by: Magazines, journalism, architecture, high-end agencies
Psychology: High contrast = clarity + authority; minimal color = confidence
Avoid: Gradients, more than 1 accent color, decorative backgrounds
```

## COLOR PSYCHOLOGY RULES

### Simultaneous Contrast
Adjacent colors shift each other's perception:
- Warm near cool = both intensify
- Light near dark = both appear more extreme
- Complementary together = vibrate (use sparingly)

### Rule of Dominance
```
60% → Dominant (background, large surfaces)
30% → Supporting (cards, panels, secondary)
10% → Accent (CTAs, highlights, focus points)
```
**Never split 50/50 — always one dominant**

### Shadow Color Rule
```
NEVER: box-shadow: 0 20px 40px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.25)  [dead gray shadow]
ALWAYS tint shadows toward primary hue:
  On dark primary bg: box-shadow: 0 20px 40px rgba(124, 58, 237, 0.3)
  On warm bg:         box-shadow: 0 20px 40px rgba(196, 157, 73, 0.2)
  On light bg:        box-shadow: 0 20px 40px rgba(15, 23, 42, 0.1)
```

### Dark Mode Rule
```
Pure #000000 black backgrounds = harsh → Use #05050A or #08080F
Pure #FFFFFF white text on dark = harsh → Use #F0EEF8 or #EEF0F5
Never use gray (#808080) as text — always lean warm or cool
```

### The 3-Second Rule
In 3 seconds a visitor's brain answers:
1. What is this? (typography + imagery)
2. Is it for me? (color emotional tone)
3. Can I trust it? (contrast, spacing, social proof)

Color is answering question #2 in milliseconds. Choose accordingly.

## FORBIDDEN COLOR PATTERNS

```
❌ Purple gradient on white — overused LLM default, immediately recognizable as AI
❌ Bootstrap blue #0D6EFD on white — screams "uncustomized template"
❌ All-gray palette with zero personality — "safe" = invisible
❌ Too many colors (4+) — confusion, no emotional anchor
❌ Low contrast text for "style" — accessibility failure AND reduces conversions
❌ Same hue for primary + secondary — no tension, no visual interest
❌ Neon on white — only works on dark backgrounds
❌ Pure black shadows on light backgrounds — look dated since 2015
```
